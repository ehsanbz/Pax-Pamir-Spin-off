What do the cards refer to?

Three distinctions for reading this atlas

The region printed on a card is not the same as the geography of a person's life. A selected period of activity differs from a complete lifespan. The title of an in-game event is not necessarily the name of a specific historical occurrence.

Relationships shown as lines in the network have descriptions and sources; thematic connections between cards appear under a separate heading in each entry.

Sources, method, and the limits of knowledge

Documented research with disagreements and limits of evidence identified

The starting point is the catalogue and card images from Rally the Troops . Card titles provide evidence for selecting a subject; historical claims in the card text have not been accepted automatically. The accounts have been checked against specialist reference entries, academic articles, museum records, archival sources, and contemporary writings.

Documented

An event or relationship with an identified source. Every network line has a description and a source link. Dates retain the precision of the source; a year alone is not converted into an invented day and month.

Thematic interpretation

Context is provided for cards such as Rumor, Nation Building, or an unnamed mercenary. A thematic connection to a biography or event does not establish the designer's intention or prove historical identity.

Uncertain or disputed

Uncertainty over Akbar Khan's lifespan, the identification of figures in images, the Balkh arsenic mine, and the claim on Mir Murad Beg's card is explicitly recorded. Entries with limited evidence have not been filled out with invented stories.

How should time and place be read?

  • All dates use the Gregorian calendar. Bars for people show selected periods of activity in this narrative, rather than complete lifespans or periods of allegiance to one side.
  • The Minaret of Jam belongs to the distant background; Hayward and Panjdeh are later developments. Flashman belongs to the literary layer and has no invented historical relationships.
  • The 12 event cards can represent continuing themes; both titles appear in each entry, but no arbitrary year has been assigned to them.
  • A card's region is distinct from its actual locations; Wade, Allard, and Pottinger in particular cannot be placed geographically from their regional labels alone.
  • The map uses low-resolution Natural Earth data for orientation; it makes no claim to reconstruct historical borders, territorial ownership, or precise travel routes.
  • The network presents a selected set of documented relationships, rather than every possible connection. When a relationship's beginning and end are unknown, it remains in the network without an invented date range.

Source criticism

Contemporary British, Russian, and court writings are valuable witnesses with particular viewpoints. Their descriptions of local societies, labels such as “bandit” and “native,” hero-making, and justifications for punishment require critical scrutiny. The summaries distinguish a witness's statement, a researcher's synthesis, and this atlas's analytical interpretation. Some academic links offer only abstracts or metadata freely; no claim is made to have read their full texts.
